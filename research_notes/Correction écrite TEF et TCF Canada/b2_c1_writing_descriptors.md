## 1. CEFR Companion Volume (2020) descriptors at B1, B2, C1

### Takeaway
The CEFR progression is: B1 writes a **linear sequence** of simple points with limited linking; B2 **develops an argument systematically**, weighs pros and cons, uses a **range of linkers** and **some complex sentences**, with errors that don't cause misunderstanding; C1 writes **well-structured texts on complex subjects** with **subsidiary points, reasons and examples**, **controlled** (not just present) cohesive devices, **flexible** complex grammar, **precise and idiomatic** vocabulary, **hedging/modality**, and **rare, hard-to-spot errors**.

### Cited findings

**Overall written production**
- B1: "straightforward connected texts" on familiar subjects, by linking "a series of shorter discrete elements into a linear sequence" [verified]. — [search result reproducing CEFR descriptors, e.g. British Council Aptis Advanced report](https://www.britishcouncil.pl/sites/default/files/aptis_advanced_report.pdf); [Hueber CEFR mapping](https://exercises.hueber.de/media/36/Language%20Hub_PreIntermediate_SB_CEFR_Mapping.pdf)
- B2: "clear, detailed texts on a variety of subjects related to [their] field of interest", synthesising and evaluating information and arguments from a number of sources [verified]. — [Aptis Advanced report](https://www.britishcouncil.pl/sites/default/files/aptis_advanced_report.pdf); [MCBU B1/B2 curriculum objectives](https://yabancidiller.mcbu.edu.tr/db_images/site_204/file/B1%20GENERAL%20CURRICULUM%20OBJECTIVES.docx)
- C1 (French version): writes well-structured texts on complex subjects, underlines the salient points, and supports a point of view at length "par l'intégration d'arguments secondaires, de justifications et d'exemples pertinents" [verified via search snippet]. — [ac-grenoble CECRL scales (search snippet; host refused fetch)](https://allemand-pedagogie.web.ac-grenoble.fr/sites/default/files/media-d7/cecrl_abrege_echelles.pdf)
- C1 adds in the CV: can use the structure and conventions of a variety of genres, varying tone, style and register by addressee, text type and theme [recalled]. — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
- C2 (for the ceiling): "textes élaborés, limpides et fluides, dans un style approprié et efficace", with a logical structure that helps the reader notice the important points [verified via search snippet]. — [ac-grenoble](https://allemand-pedagogie.web.ac-grenoble.fr/sites/default/files/media-d7/cecrl_abrege_echelles.pdf)

**Reports and essays** [recalled] — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
- B1: short, simple essays on topics of interest; can summarise, report and give an opinion on accumulated factual information with some confidence.
- B2: an essay that "develops an argument systematically with appropriate highlighting of significant points and relevant supporting detail"; gives reasons for or against a point of view; explains advantages and disadvantages of options; evaluates different ideas or solutions.
- C1: clear, well-structured expositions of complex subjects, "underlining the relevant salient issues"; expands and supports points of view at some length with subsidiary points, reasons and relevant examples; can write a suitable introduction and conclusion.

**Thematic development** [recalled] — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
- B1: develops an argument well enough to be followed without difficulty most of the time; aware of the conventional structure of the text type.
- B2: develops a clear argument, expanding and supporting points of view with subsidiary points and relevant examples; evaluates advantages and disadvantages; follows the conventional structure of the task.
- C1: integrates sub-themes, develops particular points, and rounds off with an appropriate conclusion; uses the text type's conventions to hold the reader's attention.

**Coherence and cohesion**
- B2 (DELF wording of the same descriptor): "Peut relier clairement les idées exprimées sous forme d'un texte fluide et cohérent" [verified]. — [DELF B2 grid reproduced by apprendreacomprendre.com (prep site)](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)
- C1 (French CoE wording): expresses themselves on complex subjects clearly and in a well-structured way, showing "son contrôle des outils d'organisation, d'articulation et de cohésion du discours" [verified via search snippet]. — [rm.coe.int/1680492ff3 (search snippet)](https://rm.coe.int/1680492ff3)
- [recalled] B1: links a series of shorter, discrete simple elements into a connected, linear sequence; uses a limited number of cohesive devices; makes simple, logical paragraph breaks. B2: uses a variety of linking expressions efficiently to mark the relationships between ideas; may show some "jumpiness" in a long text; structures longer texts in clear, logical paragraphs. C1: "clear, smoothly flowing, well-structured" text with "controlled use of organisational patterns, connectors and cohesive devices". — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)

**General linguistic range** [recalled] — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
- B1: enough language to get by, with some hesitation and circumlocution; can explain the main points of a problem with reasonable precision.
- B2: expresses viewpoints and develops arguments without much conspicuous searching for words, "using some complex sentence forms to do so".
- C1: a broad range of complex structures used appropriately and with considerable flexibility; selects a formulation from a broad range without having to restrict what they want to say.

**Vocabulary range**
- B2 (DELF wording): "Peut utiliser une gamme assez étendue de vocabulaire en dépit de lacunes lexicales ponctuelles entraînant l'usage de périphrases" [verified]. — [apprendreacomprendre.com (prep site reproducing official grid)](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)
- [recalled] B2: can vary formulation to avoid frequent repetition, but lexical gaps can still cause hesitation and circumlocution; produces appropriate collocations of many words fairly systematically. C1: a broad lexical repertoire, gaps readily overcome; selects among several options by exploiting synonyms, even of less common words; good command of idiomatic expressions. — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)

**Vocabulary control**
- B2 (DELF wording): "un vocabulaire généralement approprié bien que des confusions et le choix de mots incorrect se produisent sans gêner la communication" [verified]. — [apprendreacomprendre.com](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)
- [recalled] B1: good control of elementary vocabulary, but major errors still occur when expressing more complex thoughts. C1: uses less common vocabulary idiomatically and appropriately; occasional minor slips, no significant vocabulary errors. — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)

**Grammatical accuracy**
- B2 (DELF wording): "A un bon contrôle grammatical. Des erreurs non systématiques peuvent encore se produire sans conduire à des malentendus" [verified]. — [apprendreacomprendre.com](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)
- [recalled] B1: reasonable accuracy in familiar contexts, with noticeable mother-tongue influence; errors occur but meaning is clear. B2 also: complex structures used "rigidly with some inaccuracy". C1: "consistently maintains a high degree of grammatical accuracy; errors are rare and difficult to spot". — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)

**Orthographic control** [recalled] — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
- B1: spelling, punctuation and layout accurate enough to be followed most of the time. B2: reasonably accurate but may show mother-tongue influence; follows standard paragraphing. C1: layout, paragraphing and punctuation consistent and helpful; spelling accurate apart from slips.
- The DELF B2 grid says the same of punctuation: relatively accurate but may be influenced by the mother tongue [verified]. — [apprendreacomprendre.com](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)

**Sociolinguistic appropriateness**
- B2 (DELF wording): "Peut adapter sa production à la situation, au destinataire et adopter le niveau d'expression formelle convenant aux circonstances" [verified]. — [apprendreacomprendre.com](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)
- B2 can "shift between registers" within written texts (as reproduced in a British Council Aptis report) [verified via search summary]. — [Aptis Advanced report](https://www.britishcouncil.pl/sites/default/files/aptis_advanced_report.pdf)
- [recalled] B1: uses the most common exponents of functions in a neutral register. C1: adjusts formality (formal, informal, colloquial) to the context and keeps a consistent register; uses language flexibly, including allusive and joking usage. — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)

**Propositional precision** (useful for hedging) [recalled] — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
- B2: passes on detailed information reliably, though the language "lacks expressive power and idiomaticity". C1: qualifies opinions precisely by degrees of certainty, belief, likelihood; uses modality to signal the strength of a claim.

**Written interaction / correspondence** [recalled] — [CEFR CV 2020](https://rm.coe.int/common-european-framework-of-reference-for-languages-learning-teaching/16809ea0d4)
- B1: conveys information and ideas on abstract and concrete topics, gets across the point they feel is important. B2: expresses news and views effectively and relates to those of others; conveys degrees of emotion and personal significance. C1: clarity and precision, relating to the addressee flexibly and effectively.

### Inferences (reviewer-actionable, the researcher's synthesis)
- The jump **B1 → B2** is from *listing* to *arguing*: a reviewer should flag paragraphs that state an opinion with no reason, or reasons with no example, and flag essays that never weigh the other side.
- The jump **B2 → C1** is from *present* to *controlled*: B2 has connectors, complex sentences and range, C1 uses them precisely, varies them, nuances claims (modality, concession), and avoids repetition by synonymy and reformulation. Feedback for a B2 writer aiming at C1 should be about **precision and nuance**, not about adding more connectors.
- "Errors that don't cause misunderstanding" (B2) vs "rare and hard to spot" (C1) means at C1 even systematic agreement or preposition errors are level-limiting. Ink's per-category memory maps directly onto this distinction: a category the writer keeps repeating is "systematic", which the B2 descriptor explicitly allows only as non-systematic.

## 2. CLB / NCLC writing levels 7–10

### Takeaway
CLB 7–8 (adequate/fluent intermediate) are about **good paragraphs with an introduction, development and conclusion**, **appropriate connectives**, and **adequate → good control of complex structures**, with wording that may still sound like the first language. CLB 9–10 (initial/developing advanced) shift to **organisation of ideas across a longer text**, **good control of a range of complex and diverse structures**, **abstract and idiomatic vocabulary**, and **some → occasional errors in grammar, word combinations and word choice**, with limited flexibility of tone and style.

### Cited findings (all verbatim from the CLB 2012 writing profiles, extracted from the PDFs)
Source for CLB 5–8: [CLB 2012, Profiles of Ability Across Stage II Writing (copy hosted by english4real.com)](https://english4real.com/asset/clb/pdf/122.pdf). Source for CLB 9–12: [CLB 2012, Profiles of Ability Across Stage III Writing](https://english4real.com/asset/clb/pdf/134.pdf). Both are copies of the Centre for Canadian Language Benchmarks / CIC document (October 2012 edition), not the official language.ca file.

- **CLB 7, Adequate Intermediate.** Global: "Write clear, moderately complex texts on familiar concrete topics" in predictable, practical contexts. Strengths and limitations: "Good paragraph structure, with clearly expressed main ideas and adequate supporting details"; "Paragraphs are developed and joined appropriately to form a coherent text"; "Text contains an introduction, development of ideas and conclusion"; "Good range of vocabulary for moderately complex texts"; "Adequate control of complex structures"; "Wording may still be typical of first language and seem somewhat unnatural"; register "mostly appropriate for the audience".
- **CLB 8, Fluent Intermediate.** Global: adds "some abstract topics". Strengths: "good supporting details"; "Very good range of vocabulary for moderately complex texts"; "Good control of complex structures"; "Occasionally, wording may seem awkward or unnatural"; register and variety "appropriate for the audience"; "an expanded range of natural idiomatic language".
- **CLB 6, for contrast (below target).** "Appropriate use of connective words and phrases"; "Good range of vocabulary for simple everyday texts"; "Developing control of complex structures"; "Some awkward-sounding phrases and word combinations"; "Content and language are sometimes not appropriate for the audience".
- **CLB 9, Initial Advanced.** Global: "formal and informal texts of some complexity" in "routine but demanding situations", on "abstract and unfamiliar topics", for a defined audience, up to about 1,500 words. Strengths: "Adequate organization of ideas and development of topic"; "Main ideas are clearly conveyed and adequately supported with details"; "Good control of a range of complex and diverse structures"; "Some errors in grammar, word combinations and word choices still occur"; "Flexibility of tone and style may be limited"; "A good range of concrete, abstract and idiomatic language suited to context and purpose".
- **CLB 10, Developing Advanced.** "Clear organization of ideas and development of topic"; main ideas "well supported with details"; "Occasional errors in grammar, word combinations and word choices still occur"; "Flexibility of tone and style may be limited"; "A very good range of concrete, abstract and idiomatic language".
- **CLB 11 (ceiling, for contrast).** "Coherent synthesis of extensive complex information from multiple sources"; "A few minor errors"; "Flexibility of tone and style are evident".
- CLB has 12 levels per skill; Stage II = 5–8, Stage III = 9–12. — [CLB-OSA overview](https://www.clb-osa.ca/benchmarks/overview)
- IRCC TCF Canada writing conversion (as reported by a prep site): NCLC 7 = 10–11/20, NCLC 8 = 12–13, NCLC 9 = 14–15, NCLC 10+ = 16–20. — [Global Exam (prep site; verify against IRCC)](https://global-exam.com/blog/fr/tcf-canada-le-score-exact-a-atteindre-competence-par-competence/)

### Inferences
- CLB's most reviewer-actionable line is the split between **"word combinations"** (collocations) and **"word choices"**: CLB 9–10 still expect errors in both, so collocation feedback ("prendre une décision", not "faire une décision") is the right focus for NCLC 8 → 10 candidates.
- "Wording … typical of first language" (CLB 7) → "occasionally awkward" (CLB 8) → idiomatic language (CLB 9–10) is the calque axis: calque correction (section 4) is level-relevant, not cosmetic.
- "Flexibility of tone and style may be limited" persists through CLB 10, so register consistency matters more than stylistic variety at the levels TEF candidates target.

### Gaps
- The French **NCLC 2012** document's own writing descriptors (not a translation of CLB; written in parallel) were not retrieved. The CCLB annotated-grid PDF fetched only contained NCLC 5 tasks. — [CCLB grilles annotées](https://elbpld.language.ca/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Grilles-dévaluation-annotées.pdf)
- No official CLB↔CEFR mapping was retrieved; commonly cited correspondences (CLB 7 ≈ B2, CLB 9–10 ≈ C1) are unverified here.

## 3. DELF B2 / DALF C1 grids and FLE guidance for moving from B2 to C1

### Takeaway
The DELF B2 grid already rewards **nuance (concession, counter-arguments)**, **varied connectors used for their actual logical function**, a **consistent formal register**, **precision over rare words**, and **complex subordination (dont, bien que + subjonctif, gérondif, hypothèses)**. C1 asks for the same criteria at a level of control where these are consistent; the classical **thèse / antithèse / synthèse** plan, with an impersonal academic stance, is the taught structure for the C1 essai argumenté.

### Cited findings

**New FEI grids (DELF/DALF)**
- New FEI grids were officially launched 1 September (2022), refined to a reliable version in March 2022 in line with ALTE recommendations; only five or six criteria, common to all six levels, are now assessed. — [FEI, Lettre FEI 2022-10](https://www.france-education-international.fr/actualites/lettre-fei/2022-10/delf-dalf-de-nouvelles-grilles-pour-une-evaluation-au-plus-pres-des-competences-des-candidats) (via search summary; page blocked to fetch). A prep site dates the revision to 2020: conflicting. — [fle.re](https://www.fle.re/blog/delf-b2-grilles-evaluation/)
- DALF C1 grid (synthèse) criteria: pragmatic competence (length, objectivity rule, task completion, coherence and cohesion) and linguistic competence (lexique, morphosyntaxe), bands from "non répondu" to "C1+", double marking compulsory. — [FEI grille PE C1 (via search summary; page blocked)](https://france-education-international.fr/document/grille-pe-c1)
- New DELF B2 PE grid (prep-site description): 25 points, five criteria scored 0/1/3/5, where 1 = below target ("looks more like B1"), 3 = B2, 5 = B2+. Criteria: Réalisation de la tâche; Cohérence et cohésion; Adéquation sociolinguistique; Lexique; Morphosyntaxe. — [fle.re (prep site)](https://www.fle.re/blog/delf-b2-grilles-evaluation/)
  - B2+ on task requires nuance such as counter-arguments or concession: "au niveau B2, on attend que vous sachiez nuancer votre position". — [fle.re (prep site)](https://www.fle.re/blog/delf-b2-grilles-evaluation/)
  - Connectors must fit the logic, not be piled up; a formal register kept throughout, courteous even in disagreement; lexique: precision is favoured over rare words used incorrectly; periphrases for missing words are valued; morphosyntaxe: varied structures (passive, relative, subordinate). — [fle.re (prep site)](https://www.fle.re/blog/delf-b2-grilles-evaluation/)
  - 124 words or fewer (under 50% of ~250) gets 0 on all criteria. — [fle.re (prep site)](https://www.fle.re/blog/delf-b2-grilles-evaluation/)

**Old (pre-2022) DELF B2 grid, official descriptors with prep-site annotations** — [apprendreacomprendre.com](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)
- Criteria and points: correction sociolinguistique (2), capacité à présenter des faits (3), capacité à argumenter une prise de position (3), cohérence et cohésion (4), étendue du vocabulaire (2), maîtrise du vocabulaire (2), maîtrise de l'orthographe (1), choix des formes (4), degré d'élaboration des phrases (2).
- Argumentation descriptor: "Peut développer une argumentation en soulignant de manière appropriée les points importants et les détails pertinents."
- Sentence complexity descriptor: "Peut utiliser de manière appropriée des constructions variées." The annotator's scale (prep-site, not official): 1 point for *qui, que, parce que, si, quand*; 2 points for *dont, comme, puisque, bien que* and other subordinate clauses in the subjunctive; "un plus" for hypotheses, gérondif, complex relatives, cause/consequence/purpose/condition clauses.
- Annotator's examples of English-driven vocabulary errors: "nous mettons" for *to meet* (→ nous nous rencontrons), "je travel" for *voyager*; words from another language cost points (prep site).
- Annotator's punctuation advice: comma after a fronted time expression and after sentence-initial connectors, no comma before a subordinate clause (prep site).
- Annotator's grammar baseline for B2: concordance of the conditional, the subjunctive, past-participle agreement, and opinion frames (*je pense que, je suis d'avis que*) must be mastered (prep site).

**C1 essai argumenté structure (university course material)**
- The DALF C1 written paper: a synthèse of documents totalling about 1,000 words, then an essai argumenté based on the documents' content, 2h30, 25 points. — [Sorbonne Université, descriptif DALF C1](https://q-lettres.dsi.sorbonne-universite.fr/sites/default/files/media/2021-02/descriptif_DALF_C1.pdf) (via search summary)
- The essai argumenté is academic: no first-person pronoun and no examples from one's personal life (per a university methodology handout drawing on *Alter Ego C1-C2*, Hachette 2010, pp. 102–103). The dialectical plan is thèse, antithèse, synthèse, where the synthèse reaches a more nuanced middle position or goes beyond the apparent contradiction with new elements; a thematic plan suits questions that are not an opposition of ideas. — [Università di Trieste, Méthodologie : essai argumenté](https://moodle2.units.it/pluginfile.php/796273/mod_resource/content/1/3.%20M%C3%A9thodologie%2C%20essai%20argument%C3%A9.docx) (via search summary)
- Logical articulators are the means of linking ideas in a written production. — [Centre universitaire de Mila course](https://elearning.centre-univ-mila.dz/a2024/mod/resource/view.php?id=32447) (via search summary)

### Inferences (the researcher's synthesis; teachable rewrites, not sourced to a specific document)
These are standard FLE practice and consistent with the descriptors above, but the specific guidance pages (CCDMD, UdeM Centre de communication écrite, TV5Monde, Le français dans le monde) could not be retrieved, so treat them as unsourced:
- **Connectors by function** (B2 → C1 means choosing by function, varying, and not stacking): addition (*de plus, par ailleurs, en outre*), opposition/concession (*cependant, néanmoins, certes… mais, bien que + subj., quand bien même + cond.*), cause (*puisque, dans la mesure où, étant donné que*), consequence (*par conséquent, si bien que, de sorte que*), purpose (*afin que + subj.*), illustration (*ainsi, à titre d'exemple*), conclusion (*en définitive, somme toute*). A reviewer should flag a connector whose logic doesn't match (e.g. *donc* introducing an objection) and runs of the same connector.
- **Nominalisation** (C1 density): "Le gouvernement a décidé de réduire les impôts, ce qui a augmenté…" → "La réduction des impôts décidée par le gouvernement a entraîné…". Useful in topic sentences and the synthèse.
- **Precise verbs for être/avoir/faire/il y a**: "il y a beaucoup de problèmes" → "de nombreux problèmes se posent / surgissent"; "faire une étude" → "mener / réaliser une étude"; "avoir un impact" → "influer sur, peser sur"; "c'est important" → "cela s'avère essentiel".
- **Concession + nuance** is the clearest B2+/C1 signal per the grids: "Certes, X ; il n'en demeure pas moins que Y", modal hedges (*il semblerait que, dans une certaine mesure*).
- **Avoiding repetition**: pronouns (*en, y, celui-ci*), hyperonyms and synonyms, nominal anaphora (*cette mesure, ce phénomène*).

### Gaps
- Official full-text FEI B2 and C1 grid descriptors (2022) could not be fetched (FEI blocks bots). The FEI C1 advice page also blocked.
- No sourced FLE document on nominalisation, "verbes passe-partout" or connector typology was retrieved; the UdeM CCE, CCDMD (Amélioration du français) and Paris 1 "Rédiger un texte argumenté en français" (LFDM, login-gated) are the next places to look.

## 4. Typical weaknesses of English-speaking adult learners in French argumentative writing

### Takeaway
The best-attested problem areas are **gender assignment and agreement**, **prepositions**, **verb forms and past participles**, **possessives**, **spelling and accents**, and **lexical/syntactic calques** (false friends, English word order, literal idioms). Argument-level weaknesses specific to anglophones were not found in academic sources.

### Cited findings
- In a study of advanced learners' writing (ReCALL, Cambridge, 2004), the changes learners made after corpus consultation fell, by frequency, into grammatical errors (gender and agreement, prepositions, verb forms, negation and syntax), then spelling and accents, then lexico-grammatical problems, then capitalisation. — [Corpus consultation and advanced learners' writing skills in French](https://dx.doi.org/10.1017/S0958344004001211) (via search summary)
- A 70,000-word corpus of anglophone learners' written French was analysed for spelling errors, gender assignment and gender/number agreement (Springer, 1990s; authors not shown in preview). — [unpaywall link to Springer chapter](https://unpaywall.org/10.1007%2F3-540-55606-0_44) (via search summary)
- An MA thesis (University of Lethbridge, 2022) on intermediate anglophone learners attributes errors with possessive pronouns and past participles to both interlinguistic interference from English and intralinguistic overgeneralisation. — [Owusu-Ware, OPUS Lethbridge](https://opus.uleth.ca/items/294f433b-8e66-4951-bbd3-eb8a473dcbe9)
- Preposition errors in anglophone learners' written French were analysed in a Ghanaian corpus study. — [Kaiza, UG Space 2021](https://ugspace.ug.edu.gh/items/4d52b018-dcd2-4553-9beb-013ac8d29894)
- OQLF typology of English borrowings, with corrections (authoritative, Québec):
  - **Semantic** borrowings: French words given an English meaning, e.g. *réaliser* in the sense of "se rendre compte" (from *to realize*).
  - **Syntactic**: English structure transposed, e.g. *les prochaines trois semaines* → *les trois prochaines semaines*; *gardez la ligne* → *restez en ligne*.
  - **Idiomatic**: word-for-word idioms, e.g. *mettre la charrette devant le cheval* → *mettre la charrue devant les bœufs*.
  - **Morphological**: French parts on an English model (*bar laitier* from *dairy bar*).
  - **Hybrid** forms (*performer*), **integral** borrowings, pseudo-anglicisms. — [OQLF, Typologie des emprunts](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/25444/les-emprunts-a-langlais/typologie-des-emprunts)
- The OQLF's BDL prefers "emprunt à l'anglais" to "anglicisme" because not every borrowing is objectionable; acceptability depends on age of the usage, fit with the French system, and how established it is. — [OQLF, L'emprunt linguistique](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/25442/les-emprunts-a-langlais/lemprunt-linguistique-definition-contexte-et-traitement); [OQLF FAQ](https://vitrinelinguistique.oqlf.gouv.qc.ca/25443/les-emprunts-a-langlais/questions-frequentes-sur-lemprunt-linguistique)
- DELF B2 annotator examples of English interference: "mettre" for *to meet*, "travel" for *voyager* (prep site). — [apprendreacomprendre.com](http://www.apprendreacomprendre.com/files/Grille-d-evaluation-production-ecrite-B2.pdf)

### Inferences
- For a Canadian TEF/TCF audience, the OQLF is the right authority for calque judgments, and its acceptability criteria mean a reviewer should not flag every borrowing (precision over recall, consistent with Ink's AI rules).
- Ink's existing categories (Prepositions, Agreement, Conjugation, Spelling) match the top grammatical categories in the 2004 study. A "Word choice / calque" category would cover the CLB "word combinations and word choices" axis and the OQLF semantic/syntactic types, which today have no category.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed source quantifying argument-level weaknesses of anglophones (e.g. English essay habits like a thesis-first five-paragraph structure, or first-person opinion in a French academic essai) was found.
- No corpus study found that ranks false friends against gender and preposition errors for English speakers.
